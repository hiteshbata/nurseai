# 08 — Competitor Analysis

**Method note.** This is an informed structural assessment based on how these players are
positioned and how sites of their type typically perform. **Before acting on it, spend one month
of an Ahrefs or Semrush trial** and export, for each competitor: total organic traffic, top 200
keywords, referring domains, top pages by traffic, and content gaps against your domain. That is
~4 hours of work and it converts every "likely" below into a fact. Budget for one month, not a
subscription — you need the baseline once, then quarterly.

---

## 1. OET Official (oet.com — Cambridge Boxhill Language Assessment)

**What they do well**
They own the test. Every regulator, every university, every recruiter links to them. They control
the authoritative answer to every factual question: dates, fees, format, results, requirements.
Their Preparation Portal sells official practice material with unassailable credibility.

**SEO strengths**
- Overwhelming brand authority and the largest referring-domain profile in the niche by an order
  of magnitude — regulators and universities link to them by necessity.
- Owns navigational search entirely ("OET", "OET login", "OET booking", "OET results").
- Definitive answers to factual queries, which Google and AI engines default to for anything
  regulatory.
- Multi-country site structure and genuine institutional trust signals.

**Weaknesses**
- **Institutional, not helpful.** Their content answers "what are the rules", never "how do I
  actually get better". A nurse who has read the official format page still has no idea how to
  raise a Writing grade from C+ to B.
- Thin long-tail coverage. They do not write "OET speaking role play for cardiology nurses" or
  "why nurses fail writing" — those queries are wide open.
- No specialty depth, no country-of-origin content, no emotional register. A test owner cannot say
  "here's why you failed".
- Practice material is paid, limited, and not indexable.
- Slow-moving. Institutional sites ship content quarterly, not weekly.

**Opportunities for SpeakOET**
- **Never compete on facts — compete on transformation.** Cite them as the source (which builds
  your trust) and own every "how do I improve X" query.
- Own the entire long tail they ignore: specialty, source country, sub-test diagnosis, retake
  strategy.
- Be the free-practice layer. They sell official tests; you give away an AI-scored mock. Different
  value proposition, no direct collision.
- Their factual pages are boring and rarely updated. Your "verified against OET, July 2026" pages
  with answer-first formatting can outrank them in AI Overviews for *derived* questions
  ("what OET score do I need for NMC") even while they own the *primary* question ("what is OET").

## 2. E2Language (e2language.com / E2 Test Prep)

**What they do well**
The most sophisticated marketing operation in the test-prep space. Multi-test platform (OET, PTE,
IELTS, TOEFL) so their content budget amortises across four markets. Huge YouTube presence with
named, charismatic teachers. Live classes plus recorded content plus a practice platform. Strong
brand recall — many nurses know "E2" before they know "OET prep" as a category.

**SEO strengths**
- Large content library across multiple test brands; genuine topical authority.
- Massive YouTube channel feeding both search and AI-citation surfaces. Teacher-led video is their
  moat and it works.
- Established domain authority, years of accumulated links.
- Aggressive coverage of head commercial terms across four tests.

**Weaknesses**
- **Diluted focus.** OET is one of four products, and within OET, nursing is one of twelve
  professions. Nothing on their site is written specifically for a Filipino ICU nurse.
- Human-teacher model does not scale and is expensive; their pricing is a genuine vulnerability
  against an AI-first product.
- Feedback latency — human-marked writing takes days. You return it in seconds.
- Multi-test site architecture means their OET section competes internally for authority.
- Brand-search dependent, which is exactly what a "[brand] alternative" page exploits.

**Opportunities for SpeakOET**
- **`/compare/e2language-alternative` is one of the highest-ROI pages in this entire plan.**
  Searchers on that query are already in-market, already price-sensitive, and comparing. Be
  scrupulously fair — acknowledge what E2 does better (live human teachers, multi-test) and win on
  instant feedback, price, and nurse-specific depth. Unfair comparison pages lose trust and don't
  convert.
- Out-specialise them. They can never justify a page for 40 nursing specialties. You can generate
  them.
- Compete on feedback speed, which is a structural advantage, not a marketing claim.
- Their video dominance is real — do not fight it head-on. Build video for the ~20 highest-volume
  topics only, and win the other 350 pages on text and product.

## 3. Swoosh English (swooshenglish.com)

**What they do well**
The closest competitor to your positioning and the one to study hardest. Explicitly nurse-focused,
warm and personality-driven brand, very strong Facebook/community presence, heavy free content on
social. They understand the emotional reality of a nurse preparing to emigrate better than anyone
else in the market — that is their real asset.

**SEO strengths**
- Tightly focused on OET for healthcare, so their topical relevance is concentrated rather than
  diluted.
- Strong branded search from their social following.
- Community-driven distribution: their audience shares content organically, which produces natural
  links and social signals.
- Content genuinely speaks to nurses, which means better engagement metrics.

**Weaknesses**
- **Social-first, not search-first.** Enormous amounts of their best content lives in Facebook
  groups, YouTube and Instagram — invisible to Google and largely invisible to AI crawlers. This
  is a structural gap you can exploit immediately.
- Likely modest on-site content depth and technical SEO relative to their brand strength — the
  common pattern for community-led education brands.
- Course-based delivery: cohorts, schedules, live sessions. No always-on self-serve practice.
- No AI product. Their scale is bounded by teacher hours.
- Their reliance on Facebook is a platform risk they do not control.

**Opportunities for SpeakOET**
- **Convert their social content categories into indexed pages.** Whatever they post to Facebook
  about OET Writing, you publish as a properly structured, schema-marked, answer-first page. You
  capture the search demand their content creates but does not serve.
- Beat them on technical foundation — that is a cheap, decisive win.
- Compete on availability: a nurse on night shifts in Kerala can practise at 3am. A cohort course
  cannot serve that.
- Do **not** try to out-warm them on brand voice; they will win that. Win on product depth,
  instant feedback and search coverage.
- Watch their pricing closely — you are targeting the same wallet.

## 4. Benchmark Education

**What they do well**
Long-established, Australia-anchored, credible with AHPRA-bound candidates. Structured
curriculum, teacher-led, institutional relationships with colleges and agencies. Trusted by
recruiters, which is a distribution channel you should want.

**SEO strengths**
- Domain age and accumulated authority.
- Strong for Australia-specific OET queries and AHPRA-pathway content.
- Institutional links from Australian education and migration ecosystems.

**Weaknesses**
- Geographically narrow — Australia-centric content, weak on UK/NMC, Ireland and Gulf pathways.
- Traditional course business; likely dated site experience and slow content velocity.
- Little to no programmatic or long-tail coverage.
- Weak in source-country content (India, Philippines, Nigeria) despite that being where the
  candidates are.

**Opportunities for SpeakOET**
- **Own the UK/NMC corridor decisively** — larger candidate volume than Australia and less well
  served by them.
- Own source-country content: `/oet/from-india/to-australia` competes directly with their
  destination-only framing, from the searcher's actual point of view.
- Their agency and college relationships are a template. Copy the *motion* (Tier 3/4 in
  [07](07-digital-pr.md)), not the content.

## 5. OET Online / smaller prep sites

**What they do well**
Fast, cheap, focused. Usually one or two people producing practice tests and PDFs. Some rank
surprisingly well on long-tail "free OET practice test" queries because they have been publishing
consistently for years.

**SEO strengths**
- Long-tail coverage of "free OET [sub-test] practice test PDF" — high-volume, high-intent queries.
- Low overhead means they can publish constantly.
- Some have real domain age.

**Weaknesses**
- Poor UX, ad-heavy, often outdated material.
- No scoring, no feedback, no product — just static PDFs and answer keys.
- Weak technical SEO, poor mobile performance, no schema.
- No brand, no trust signals, no author credibility.
- Highly exposed to Google's quality updates; many of these sites have already lost traffic.

**Opportunities for SpeakOET**
- **This is your easiest traffic to take.** They rank for "free OET practice test" with a static
  PDF. You can rank for the same query with an *interactive, AI-scored* test. Better user signals,
  better content, better product — you should win these within 6 months.
- Match their coverage of free-resource queries, then win on quality. Every one of these queries
  belongs in Wave 1.
- Their users are actively looking for something better. The switching cost is zero.

## The four wedges, in priority order

1. **Specialty depth.** Nobody serves "OET speaking practice for a cardiology nurse". You can
   generate 280 such pages, each with a working role-play. Structurally uncontested.
2. **Instant AI feedback.** Every competitor's writing feedback takes days and costs teacher hours.
   Yours takes seconds and costs cents. This is a real product advantage, not positioning — lead
   every landing page with it.
3. **Source-country framing.** Competitors write from the destination's perspective ("OET for
   Australia"). Nurses search from their own ("OET for Indian nurses going to UK"). Write from the
   searcher's side.
4. **AI-search presence.** None of these five is optimised for ChatGPT/Perplexity citation. That
   window is open right now and it will close within 18 months as the category matures.

## What to monitor quarterly

- Ahrefs/Semrush: their new pages and new keywords — this tells you what is working in the niche
  without you paying to learn it.
- Their pricing pages (screenshot them; pricing moves are strategy signals).
- Swoosh's Facebook group and E2's YouTube uploads — their content calendar is your keyword
  research, and it is free.
- Whether any of them launches an AI feature. When one does, your differentiation shifts from
  "we have AI" to "our AI is better for nurses" — and your specialty and country depth become the
  defence. Build that depth *before* you need it.
