# 10 — KPIs & Targets

## How to read these

Targets assume: a brand-new domain, the [05](05-technical-roadmap.md) roadmap executed in weeks
1–4, 2–4 articles/week sustained, programmatic launching week 6, and the digital-PR motion in
[07](07-digital-pr.md) running from month 2.

**Two honest caveats up front.**

1. **New-domain lag is real.** Months 1–3 will look like nothing is working. Indexation is slow,
   rankings are volatile, and traffic is a trickle. This is normal and is not a signal to change
   strategy. The inflection typically lands in month 4–6. If you abandon the plan in month 3
   because the numbers are small, you will have paid the entire cost and collected none of the
   return.
2. **"Domain authority" is a third-party metric, not a Google one.** Track it because it is a
   convenient proxy for link growth, but never optimise for it. Referring domains and rankings are
   the real numbers.

**Month-1 success is defined technically, not commercially:** every blog post in the sitemap,
`/pricing` indexable, schema live, SSR verified, Search Console and Bing verified, internal-link
system shipped. If those are done, month 1 succeeded regardless of the traffic number.

## About the 4,000 target

**Organic SEO alone will not deliver 4,000 paying subscribers in 12 months from a new domain.**
That is arithmetic, not pessimism — see the funnel math in the [README](README.md). A realistic
organic-only outcome is 1,200–1,900.

**4,000 total is achievable in 12–14 months if the four loops run together:**

| Channel | Cumulative paid subs by month 12 | Notes |
|---|---|---|
| Organic search | 1,200–1,900 | This plan. Compounds, no ongoing spend. |
| Referral (`/refer`, already built) | 700–1,100 | Nurses prepare in cohorts. Highest-leverage loop you own. |
| YouTube + Instagram | 600–1,000 | Accounts exist. OET is a performance skill; video converts. |
| Partnerships (agencies, colleges) | 300–600 | One agency can send hundreds. B2B revenue too. |
| Paid ads (small budget, retargeting) | 200–400 | Retarget organic traffic only — cheapest paid you can buy |
| **Total** | **3,000–5,000** | Midpoint ≈ 4,000 |

**The strategic point:** SEO's job is to fill the top of the funnel cheaply and permanently. The
referral loop multiplies it. Do not judge SEO against 4,000 — judge it against 1,500 and against
whether it is *still* producing subscribers in month 24 with no additional spend. That is what no
other channel does.

## The weekly dashboard (5 numbers, not 50)

Check these every Monday. Everything else is quarterly.

1. **Organic sessions**, week over week.
2. **Indexed pages** vs submitted (Search Console → Pages). A widening gap is the earliest warning
   of a content-quality problem.
3. **Signups from organic**, and the top 5 landing pages producing them.
4. **New paying subscribers, by first-touch landing page.** The only number that pays salaries.
5. **New referring domains**, this week.

## The monthly review (add 4)

6. Top 20 pages by impressions with CTR below 2% — these need better titles, not more content.
7. Pages with impressions but zero clicks — ranking positions 11–20, the cheapest wins available.
8. Programmatic indexation rate, per template.
9. The 25-prompt AI panel.

## The quarterly review

- Full technical crawl (Screaming Frog): 4xx, orphans, duplicate titles, canonical errors.
- Core Web Vitals field data (CrUX), mobile only.
- Competitor delta in Ahrefs: their new pages and keywords.
- **Prune:** any page with zero impressions after 90 days gets improved or `noindex`ed.
- **Refresh:** every page containing a fee, score requirement or process gets verified.

## Leading vs lagging indicators

Founders panic because they watch lagging indicators. Watch the leading ones instead — they move
6–12 weeks earlier.

**Leading (act on these):** pages published/week · indexation rate · internal links per page ·
new referring domains · AI-crawler hits in Vercel logs · impressions in Search Console.

**Lagging (report, don't react):** organic sessions · rankings · paid subscribers · domain rating.

If impressions are rising while clicks are flat, you have a title/CTR problem, not a content
problem. If indexation rate is falling while pages published rises, you have a quality problem.
Both are visible in the leading indicators weeks before they show up in revenue.
