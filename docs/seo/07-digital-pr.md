# 07 — Digital PR & Link Acquisition

## The constraint that shapes everything

New domain, small budget, YMYL-adjacent niche. Two implications:

1. **You cannot buy your way to authority.** Paid links in an education/health niche are the
   fastest route to a manual action, and the guest-post farms that email you are already flagged.
2. **You have one genuine asset competitors don't: data.** Once a few thousand nurses have
   practised on SpeakOET, you can publish things nobody else can — "the sub-test nurses fail most
   often", "average band by source country", "the 20 most common errors in OET referral letters".
   Original data is the only reliable way a startup earns links from institutions.

**Target: 40–60 referring domains in 12 months**, weighted toward `.edu`, `.ac.uk`, `.org` and
established nursing publications. That is a modest number that beats 400 junk links, because in
this niche the *quality* differential between a nursing-school link and a directory link is
enormous.

---

## Tier 1 — The data-story engine (highest ROI)

**The play.** Publish one original research piece per quarter, built from your own anonymised
platform data, and pitch it to nursing media and educators.

**Q1 — "The OET Writing Report 2026"**
Analysis of N referral letters submitted to SpeakOET. Findings: which of the 6 criteria nurses
lose most marks on, the 20 most common language errors, how scores differ by source country.
Include real (anonymised) letter excerpts and a downloadable one-page summary.
*Pitchable to:* Nursing Times, Nursing Standard, NurseJournal, university ESL/EAP departments,
OET-focused YouTubers.

**Q2 — "Which OET Sub-Test Do Nurses Actually Fail?"**
Cross-sub-test pass-rate data from your mock tests, segmented by country. Publish the dataset as
CSV with a `Dataset` schema. Datasets get cited by academics, and academic citations are `.edu`
links.

**Q3 — "The Pronunciation Report: Which Sounds Cost Nurses Marks"**
You have pronunciation analysis — nobody else in this market does. Break down intelligibility
issues by L1 (Malayalam, Tagalog, Hindi, Yoruba, Nepali). This is genuinely novel linguistic data
and applied-linguistics departments will link to it.

**Q4 — "OET Preparation Costs: A Global Survey"**
Survey 500 users on total spend (course fees, retakes, materials, travel). Cost stories get picked
up because they are concrete and shareable.

**Why this works:** journalists and educators link to *numbers*, not opinions. Each report is
2–3 weeks of work and should produce 8–15 referring domains, plus AI-search citations that last
years, plus social content, plus sales collateral. Nothing else in this document has that
multiplier.

**Execution:** publish the report on-site first at a permanent URL, with schema, a summary
infographic, and an explicit "cite this research" block containing a pre-formatted citation and
the canonical link. Then pitch. Never pitch before the page is live.

## Tier 2 — Free tools as link assets

Tools earn links passively and forever. Your calculator will be linked by people you never contact.

**Make them linkable by design:**
- Every tool gets a permanent, clean URL and works with no signup.
- Add an **embed widget**: `<iframe>` + copy-paste snippet with an attribution backlink. Nursing
  bloggers embed calculators; each embed is a link.
- Add a "cite this tool" block.
- Ship a **free downloadable resource** alongside: OET writing checklist PDF, 500-word vocabulary
  list, 30-day study planner. Resource pages on university sites link to downloadable PDFs far
  more readily than to product pages.

**Then do the low-tech outreach that actually converts:** find every existing "OET resources" page
on a nursing school or ESL site (`site:.edu "OET" resources`, `site:.ac.uk "OET"`), and email the
page owner with the specific tool that fits their list. Not "please link to us" — "your resources
page lists three OET links, one of which is dead; here's a free calculator your students can use".
Expect a 5–10% response rate, which at 200 emails is 10–20 links.

## Tier 3 — Nursing colleges and universities

The most valuable links available, and the slowest.

**Who to approach**
- Nursing colleges in Kerala, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, Punjab, Delhi NCR (India); Manila, Cebu,
  Davao (Philippines); Lagos, Abuja (Nigeria); Kathmandu (Nepal).
- UK/Australian universities running OSCE-prep or international-nurse bridging programmes.
- ESL/EAP departments teaching healthcare English.

**What to offer — in order of what actually gets accepted**
1. **Free institutional access.** Give a college 50 free accounts for a semester. In return, ask
   to be listed on their student-resources page. This is a real value exchange, not a link
   purchase, and it is how education partnerships normally work.
2. **A free webinar** for final-year students on OET preparation. The event page links to you; the
   recording links to you; the students become users.
3. **A scholarship.** 20 free annual subscriptions awarded by application. Scholarship pages on
   university sites are a legitimate, long-standing link source — but only if the scholarship is
   real, awarded, and announced. A fake scholarship page is a spam signal and an ethical problem.
4. **Guest lecture / curriculum contribution.** Slowest, strongest. A named faculty relationship
   produces links, testimonials, referrals and credibility simultaneously.

**Pitch template (adapt, do not send verbatim):**
> Subject: Free OET practice access for your final-year nursing students
>
> Dear [Name],
>
> I'm [Founder], and I built SpeakOET — an AI OET practice platform for nurses — after seeing how
> many Indian nurses fail the Writing sub-test on a first attempt despite strong clinical English.
>
> I'd like to offer [College] 50 free student accounts for the coming semester, with no
> conditions. If it's useful to your students, I'd be glad for it to appear on your resources
> page; if not, they keep the access anyway.
>
> Happy to run a free 45-minute session for your cohort on OET Writing if that's helpful.

**Realistic yield:** 100 colleges contacted → 15 replies → 6 partnerships → 4 links. Slow, but
these are the links that move a new domain.

## Tier 4 — Hospitals and healthcare employers

Lower link volume, higher commercial value.

- **International recruitment teams** at NHS trusts, Australian health services and Gulf hospital
  groups all run OET-support programmes for candidates they've sponsored. They publish candidate
  resource pages. Offer a discounted cohort licence — this is B2B revenue *and* a link.
- **Recruitment agencies** placing nurses in the UK/Ireland/Australia. They want their candidates
  to pass OET faster (they don't get paid until the nurse registers). Perfectly aligned incentive.
  Offer a partner programme with a co-branded landing page. Higher volume than colleges and much
  faster to close.
- **Hospital nursing-education departments** in source countries — many run in-house OET prep for
  staff planning to emigrate.

Prioritise agencies. They are commercially motivated, decide quickly, and one agency can send
hundreds of paying users. Treat the link as a bonus, not the goal.

## Tier 5 — Healthcare blogs, publications and creators

**Publications to target:** Nursing Times (UK), Nursing Standard, NurseJournal, Nurse.org,
Minority Nurse, Australian Nursing & Midwifery Journal, Philippine nursing outlets, Indian nursing
association newsletters.

**Approach:** contributed expert articles, not guest posts. Offer a genuinely useful piece —
"Five language habits that cost international nurses their OET Writing grade" — written by your
nurse-writer, with a byline and one contextual link. Real editorial contribution, not a link drop.

**Creators.** OET YouTubers and Instagram educators with 10k–100k followers. Offer free lifetime
access plus an affiliate commission. Most will take it. A single video from a mid-tier OET creator
can outperform a month of SEO in the short term — and the video description link plus the
transcript both feed AI search.

**HARO / Qwoted / Featured.** Answer journalist queries on nursing shortages, international
recruitment and healthcare workforce. Slow, free, occasionally lands a national outlet.

## Tier 6 — Communities (traffic and AEO, not links)

Reddit, Facebook groups ("Nurses going to UK", "OET Preparation Group" — some have 100k+ members),
Quora, Discord.

**Rules that keep this from backfiring:**
- Founder posts under a real, labelled identity. Never sockpuppets.
- 10 genuinely helpful answers before any product mention.
- Answer the question fully *in the comment*. Never "I wrote a blog post about this, link in bio".
- Respect each group's promotion rules; ask moderators for permission to share a free tool.

Facebook links are `nofollow` and largely invisible to crawlers — the value here is **direct
paying users and AI-search presence** (Reddit and Quora are heavily weighted in LLM retrieval, see
[04](04-aeo-geo.md)). This is the highest-conversion channel per hour in the entire plan for the
first 90 days, before SEO compounds.

## Tier 7 — Government and official resources

Realistic assessment: **you will not get a link from NMC, AHPRA or OET Official.** Do not spend
time on it.

What is achievable:
- **Citing them correctly** on your country pages, with a "verified against [source], [date]"
  line. This builds trust with humans and AI engines even without a reciprocal link.
- **Embassy and migration-authority resource lists** in source countries occasionally list
  preparation resources — worth a single check per country.
- **State nursing council newsletters** in India (Kerala, Tamil Nadu) sometimes publish
  emigration-guidance content and accept contributions.
- **Wikipedia's OET article** — do not add a promotional link, it will be reverted and it is
  against policy. But if you publish original research (Tier 1), a *third party* may cite it there
  legitimately. That is the only correct route.

---

## What NOT to do

- Paid link placements, guest-post networks, PBNs. Fastest route to a manual action in an
  education niche, and recovery takes 6–12 months you cannot afford.
- Mass automated outreach. Nursing-school administrators talk to each other.
- Comment and forum-signature spam. Zero value since 2013.
- Directory submissions beyond the ~10 legitimate SaaS directories in [04, §4](04-aeo-geo.md).
- Fake scholarships or fake research. Both are found out and both are unrecoverable in a trust
  business.
- Buying "DA 50+ backlinks" from anyone who emails you.
