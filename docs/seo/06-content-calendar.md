# 06 — 12-Month Content Roadmap

## Operating principles

1. **Publish for the buyer first, the reader second.** For the first 4 months, every article must
   be adjacent to a purchase decision. Grammar and vocabulary content is valuable but it does not
   pay salaries in month 3 — it goes in month 7+.
2. **2 articles/week is the floor, 4/week is the target** once Issue #19 (CMS migration) lands.
   Below 2/week a new domain does not build momentum fast enough to matter.
3. **Refresh beats publish after month 6.** Updating an article that ranks #8 to #3 is usually
   worth more than a new article that starts at #40.
4. **Every article ships with:** answer-first opening, 6 FAQs + schema, 3 sibling links, 1 pillar
   link, 1 conversion link, a named author, and a visible updated date.
5. **Nothing publishes without a clinical sanity check** if it contains clinical content. A wrong
   fact on a nursing site is a permanent trust loss.

---

## The ranking model

Every topic scored on two axes, which is how the calendar is ordered.

- **Traffic potential (T):** 5 = 5,000+/mo achievable · 4 = 1,000–5,000 · 3 = 300–1,000 ·
  2 = 100–300 · 1 = <100
- **Conversion potential (C):** 5 = searcher buys within days · 4 = weeks · 3 = considering ·
  2 = researching · 1 = curious

**Priority score = (T × 1) + (C × 2).** Conversion is double-weighted because you need paying
subscribers, not sessions. Max score 15.

## Q4 — Months 10–12 · "Consolidate and convert"

**Goal:** 1,600 pages. Shift from volume to yield.

**Editorial (2/week new, 2/week refresh):** Grammar cluster (110–133), Vocabulary support
(134–161), remaining tools.

**The real work this quarter is optimisation, not publishing:**

1. **Prune.** Any page with zero impressions after 90 days: improve it or `noindex` it. Dead pages
   drag crawl budget and dilute topical focus.
2. **Consolidate cannibalisation.** Pages competing for the same query get merged, with a 301.
3. **Conversion-rate work.** Take the 20 highest-traffic pages and A/B test the CTA, the position
   of the lead magnet, and the offer. A 1% → 2% lift on your top 20 pages is worth more than 100
   new articles.
4. **Refresh for the new year.** Every page with a year, fee or requirement gets verified and
   re-dated in December for the January search surge.
5. **Double down.** Identify the 3 clusters producing paying subscribers and put 70% of Q1-next-
   year effort there.

## Production reality check

**A 4-articles/week schedule at 1,800 words with clinical review is roughly 20 hours/week.**
Options, in order of recommendation:

1. **Founder writes the 30 highest-conversion pages personally** (Q1). Nobody else knows the
   product or the customer well enough, and these pages carry the revenue. ~10h/week for 12 weeks.
2. **Hire one OET-qualified nurse-writer part-time from month 3** (~$800–1,500/mo in India or the
   Philippines). They give you credibility, clinical accuracy, and a named author for E-E-A-T.
   This is the single best marketing hire available at this stage.
3. **AI-assisted drafting with mandatory human editing** for programmatic content only. Never for
   the 30 money pages — those must be genuinely better than E2Language's, and generic AI prose
   will not clear that bar.

**Do not** buy generic $30 articles from a content mill. In a YMYL-adjacent niche with expert
competitors, that content will not rank, and if it contains a wrong regulatory fact it will cost
you a customer permanently.
